01 / Welcome

The AI Ecosystem
Compass for Educators

From AI adoption to institutional resilience.

AI stewardship
People · Practice · Governance · Resilience

A shared direction. Different starting points.

AI stewardship means taking responsibility for how AI is selected, used, questioned and improved in education. This ecosystem connects educator beliefs and professional skills with the institutional conditions that support responsible practice.

Follow eight connected sections to explore your perspective, listen to others and choose an action you can take. You can revisit any section at any time.

Using this workshop

Move the sliders or explicitly choose a midpoint. Unanswered questions stay unanswered. Responses save in this browser when storage is available; export a backup before closing. No responses are sent anywhere automatically.

For group work, participants export their ratings and the facilitator imports those files into the Collective Dashboard. This is a device-local workshop, without live synchronisation.

The file includes its own graphics and works without an internet connection in a browser that runs local HTML. On an iPad, a Files preview may not run the activities; use an HTML viewer or open the published website in Safari.

Facilitator opening · suggested 5 minutes

Invite disagreement and allow participants to pass. Explain who will see shared ratings. Use no names or learner details. Suggested full workshop: 75–90 minutes.

02 / Context

Why this matters

Every AI decision is also a decision about learning, trust and responsibility.

Professional judgement

When AI contributes to learning materials or feedback, educators still need to check accuracy, relevance and the impact on learners.

Trust and transparency

Making AI use visible creates space to discuss what was generated, what was verified and where human judgement shaped the result.

Institutional responsibility

Individual confidence needs support: clear ownership, agreed practices and safe ways to report concerns.

Resilience

A resilient institution can respond when a system fails, keep learning going and use the experience to improve.

The workshop asks: What kind of AI future are we shaping together?

Pair discussion · 8 minutes

Ask for one opportunity and one concern. Separate what participants have observed from what they anticipate.

03 / Capability

AI Stewardship: knowledge and skills

Responsible practice connects understanding with action.

01 · AI literacy

Understand capabilities, limitations and the context of use.

Practise: verify a claim, compare outputs, identify who may be excluded.

02 · AI governance

Make use, ownership and accountability visible.

Practise: document a tool’s purpose, data use, approval and review arrangements.

03 · AI stewardship

Apply professional judgement, oversee outputs and intervene.

Practise: explain a human review step and decide when to pause use.

04 · AI resilience

Respond to incidents, maintain alternatives and learn.

Practise: rehearse a fallback and follow a concern through to resolution.
Which capabilities would you like to develop?

Capability conversation · 8 minutes

Distinguish a training need from a missing institutional process. An individual cannot solve every governance gap through additional skills.

04 / Belief mapping

What do you believe about AI?

Make your starting point visible. Leave room for it to change.

Each slider is a spectrum between the two labelled perspectives. The middle means a balanced or context-dependent view. These are discussion prompts, with no correct answers and no overall belief score.

Compare perspectives · 12 minutes

Invite people to explain a position, then describe a context in which they would move. “Partner” describes a way of working; it does not imply that AI is human.

05 / Professional identity

Which tribe are you?

Five ways of relating to AI. A conversation about identity, not a test of competence.

You may recognise more than one tribe or move between them across situations. Each brings a useful perspective to the ecosystem.

Which best describes your position today?

Explore the Five Tribes · 10 minutes

Invite different voices. Avoid presenting the tribes as a ladder, or the Shaper as the only desirable destination.

06 / Institutional resilience

Is your institution resilient?

Look beyond individual confidence to the systems that support responsible practice.

Position each slider from Not yet established to Fully embedded. The middle means developing practice. Choose “Don’t know” when you lack evidence; uncertainty is useful to discuss.

This is a reflective workshop audit, not a validated maturity assessment or certification.

Examine readiness · 12 minutes

Ask participants to distinguish “a policy exists” from “people know how to use it”. Discuss disagreements before treating averages as an institutional picture.

07 / Governance Scenarios

AI stewardship in action

Turn principles into decisions when something goes wrong.

Reveal discussion prompts

Consider disclosure, human review, safe reporting, responsibility, recording the incident and a follow-up review. Adapt responses to your institution’s procedures.

Small-group scenario · 15 minutes

Give each group a different scenario. Ask who takes the next action, what learners need now and what would prevent recurrence. These are workshop situations, not statements of legal requirements.

08 / Reflection

Reflection and next steps

Carry one insight into a concrete change.

Your full reflection includes private notes. The separate group-sharing file below includes ratings, tribe and capability selections only.

Collective Dashboard

A conversation starter built from responses on this device. Import participant files to bring a workshop together. No live collection or automatic sharing takes place.

How to read and share the dashboard

Each bar shows the average of answered ratings only. Missing and “Don’t know” responses are excluded; the count and range reveal coverage and differences. Belief positions are not measures of quality. Audit values are perceptions, not verified institutional findings.

Ask participants to export their ratings and send the files through a channel your group agrees to use. Import them here. Re-importing a response updates that participant’s existing record. No names are requested, but small-group patterns may still be identifiable.

Imported files do not replace your own reflection. Each browser keeps a separate collection. Export collected ratings if you need to move the dashboard to another device.

Close the workshop · 10 minutes

Look for differing perspectives and unknowns as well as common ground. Agree a responsible person and review date for one institutional action.
